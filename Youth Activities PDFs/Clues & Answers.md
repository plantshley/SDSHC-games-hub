## **`💖 SOURCE: Activities - Upper Elementary (3rd-5th)`**

### **`CLORPT: Five Factors of Soil Formation (Pages 12-14)`**

* **`Climate (C):`** `Weather over a long period of time. Soils develop fastest in warm, moist climates and slowest in cold or arid (dry) ones. [CLORPT-climate]`  
* **`Organisms (O):`** `Plant roots spread, animals burrow, and bacteria eat, which changes how soil forms. These help break down soil particles. [CLORPT-organisms]`  
* **`Relief (R):`** `Refers to the shape of the land. The direction a hill faces changes how much sunlight the soil gets and how much water it holds. Deeper soils form at the bottom of a hill. [CLORPT-relief]`  
* **`Parent Material (P):`** `Describes the properties in which soil forms (e.g., weathering rock vs. a dry lake bed). [CLORPT-parent-material]`  
* **`Time (T):`** `Older soils differ from younger soils because they have had more time to develop. [CLORPT-time]`

  * **`Answer:`** `PRAIRIE [soil regions-prairie]`

  * **`Answer:`** `FORESTS [soil regions-forests]`  
* **`Clue:`** `_____ soils are found in all regions. They protect against floods and help clean out pollutants in the environment.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `WETLAND [soil regions-wetland]`  
* **`Clue:`** `Tundra soils have a frozen layer called _____ which can prevent trees and other plants from growing deep roots.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `PERMAFROST [soil regions-tundra]`  
* **`Clue:`** `Tropical soils are lush, but break down minerals and organic matter quickly and are not _____.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `FERTILE [soil regions-topical]`

### **`Soil Biology (Page 21)`**

* **`Clue:`** `Single-celled organisms that are microscopic, ranging in shape from spheres, rods, to spirals.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `BACTERIA [bacteria]`

  * **`Answer:`** `NEMATODES [nematode]`

  * **`Answer:`** `PROTOZOA [protozoa]`

  * **`Answer:`** `ROTIFERS [rotifer]`

### **`Soil Conservation Practices (Pages 11-12)`**

* **`Clue:`** `A practice that provides living roots throughout much of the year which absorbs nutrients and prevents loss. They are planted in addition to a cash crop.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `COVER CROPS [cons practices-CC]`  
* **`Clue:`** `A technology where water control structures are added to existing tile networks to raise or lower the water table to a desired elevation.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `DRAINAGE WATER MANAGEMENT [cons practices-DWM]`  
* **`Clue:`** `A technology that allows farmers to pass drainage tile water through perennial vegetation to reduce nutrient loss to local waterbodies.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `SATURATED BUFFERS [cons practices-buffer]`  
* **`Clue:`** `A line of prairie plants planted in-between row crop or along streams and waterbodies that aids in reducing erosion and nutrient runoff.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `PRAIRIE STRIPS [cons practices-prairie-strip]`  
* **`Clue:`** `An edge of field practice that filters drainage tile water through woodchips to remove nutrients and protect water quality.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `BIOREACTORS [cons practices-bioreactor]`  
* **`Clue:`** `Shallow pools that filter sediment, nitrate, and other nutrients and also provide flood mitigation and habitat benefits.`  
  * **`Answer:`** `WETLANDS [cons practices-wetland]`
