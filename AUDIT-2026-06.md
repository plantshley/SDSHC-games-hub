## Section B — Content accuracy & consistency

**Overall:** internal consistency across kid + advanced content is strong — answers match their keys, explanations are self-consistent, registries match content lengths (spot-checked: kid registry `levelCount` vs content arrays all align after the Trivia Blitz fix). The items below are the exceptions.

**B-1 Field Guide — native plants & farm equipment (web-verified vs USDA PLANTS / NRCS / USFS / SDSU Extension).**
All ~38 **farm-equipment** items: **verified accurate.** The only quibbles are *conservative* ranges, not errors (self-propelled sprayer booms listed "90–120 ft" when the largest now reach ~140 ft; large square bales "up to half a ton" when 0.25–0.75 ton is typical) — leave as-is. The ~36 **native-plant** items are also overwhelmingly accurate (botanical names, SD-specific claims, root depths all check out). Three need correction:

- **B-1a [MAJOR — factual error] ✅ DONE — Common Milkweed significance said "The sole host plant for monarch butterfly larvae on the Great Plains."** Monarchs use *all* `Asclepias` species; several others occur on the Great Plains. *A. syriaca* is a primary host, not the sole one.
  **Fix:** change "sole host plant" → **"a primary host plant."** Source: [Wildflower Center — Asclepias syriaca](https://www.wildflower.org/plants/result.php?id_plant=ASSY)
- **B-1b [MINOR — misleading clue] ✅ DONE — Ponderosa Pine** clue 2: *"Long needles grow in bundles of 2 (occasionally 3)."* In the Black Hills variety (*P. ponderosa var. scopulorum*), 2- and 3-needle bundles **commonly coexist on the same tree** — that mix is itself a diagnostic mark, so "occasionally 3" misdirects ID.
  **Fix:** **"Long needles grow in bundles of 2 or 3 — often mixed on the same branch."** Source: [USFWS — Know Your Trees: Ponderosa Pine](https://www.fws.gov/story/2024-05/know-your-trees-ponderosa-pine)
- **B-1c [MINOR — unsupported claim] ✅ DONE — Maximilian Sunflower** stated rhizomes "reaching 15 feet deep." No NRCS/USFS/botanical source corroborates a 15-ft depth (the rhizomatous habit itself is well-documented).
  **Fix:** drop the number → **"a deep, spreading rhizome network."** Source: [USDA Plant Guide — Helianthus maximiliani](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/pg_hema2.pdf)
- **B-1d [NOTE] Purple Coneflower** uses *Echinacea angustifolia* (correct for the SD/Great Plains native), but the "drooping petals" description reads more like *E. purpurea*; *E. angustifolia* rays reflex less and are narrower. Not misleading enough to change — instructors should just be aware.

*(Ball Cactus `Escobaria vivipara` is taxonomically in flux but remains the USDA-accepted name — no change needed.)*

**B-2 [MINOR] Odd One Out — coconut as "a seed."**
[odd-one-out.js:24-33](src/data/content/odd-one-out.js#L24): Broccoli, Artichoke, Coconut, Squash → answer Squash, explanation *"Broccoli and artichoke are flower buds, and coconut is a seed. Squash is a fruit!"* Botanically a coconut is a **drupe (a fruit)**, same broad category as squash. A sharp 5th-grader could argue coconut is "the fruit" too, making the odd-one-out ambiguous. Round 4 reuses the same "coconut = seed" framing. Educationally defensible (the edible part is the seed) but loose.
**Fix (optional):** swap coconut for an unambiguous seed (e.g. sunflower seed / bean) in these two rounds, or reword to "the rest are flower buds or the part we eat is a seed."

**B-3 [MINOR] Don't Belong L4 — "elements" concept for Pre-K–2nd.**
[dont-belong.js:48-59](src/data/content/dont-belong.js#L48): Grape, Water, Earth, Wind → "They're all elements!" Classical elements (water/earth/wind) vs. a grape is abstract for the Little Sprouts (Pre-K–2nd) audience this tier targets. The mechanic still works (grape is clearly the food item) but the *reveal* won't teach anything to a 5-year-old.
**Fix (optional):** reframe as "three are weather/nature, one is a snack," or replace with a concrete Pre-K grouping.

**B-4 [MINOR] Field Guide item shape is inconsistent: `image` vs `images`.**
Some items use a single `image:` string ([field-guide.js:21](src/data/content/advanced/field-guide.js#L21)), others an `images:` array ([:34-37](src/data/content/advanced/field-guide.js#L34)). This is presumably handled by the game, but it's a latent footgun (a typo'd key silently renders no photo).
**Fix:** confirm the game normalizes both; consider standardizing on `images: []`.

**B-5 [NOTE] Don't Belong reuses Field Guide `.webp` photos for soil types in L2.**
[dont-belong.js:26-29](src/data/content/dont-belong.js#L26) points sand/clay/silt/leaf at `/assets/field-guide/...webp`. Fine and DRY, but it couples a kid-mode game to advanced-mode assets — if Field Guide photos are ever reorganized, this kid level breaks. Worth a comment at minimum.

## Section E — User-experience / audience-appropriateness

**E-1 [MAJOR] ✅ DONE — Failed score save was invisible to the player.** Previously every advanced game did `recordScores(...).catch(err => console.error(...))`, so a failed/pending write still showed success. **Fixed:** all 6 games now call `recordScoresWithStatus()` ([score-save-status.js](src/utils/score-save-status.js)), which shows a non-blocking pill: **Saving score… → Score saved ✓**, or **Saved offline — will sync** (detected via `navigator.onLine`, since Firestore's offline `batch.commit()` stays pending until reconnect), or **Couldn't save score — tell an organizer** on rejection. *To verify, see [TESTING-PLAN.md](TESTING-PLAN.md) Section 2.5.*

**E-2 [MINOR] Planting Sim 5.5s completion delay.** [planting-sim.js:804](src/games/planting-sim.js#L804). Kids may tap around confused during the wait. Consider ~2.5–3s or a skippable tap.

**E-3 [NOTE] Idle timers per spec.** Kid 120s (10s warning), Advanced 600s, admin disabled. Confirm at runtime (TESTING-PLAN Section 3.3, Section 6.4) but code matches CLAUDE.md.

**E-4 [NOTE] Don't Belong is very short (4 levels).** Acceptable for Pre-K drop-in play; noted for expectations only.
